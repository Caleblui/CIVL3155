In the exam room

Spend the 180 minutes like this

  1. Planning time (10 min): read the whole paper. Circle the recycled hydrology questions; they're your fastest marks. For FSF, write down q, E and yc for each section before you're allowed to write answers.
  2. Budget about 1.7 min per mark. A 30-mark FSF question gets ~50 min; a 2-mark MCQ gets ~3 min. If you're stuck, move on.
  3. Hydrology recycled questions first (lysimeter, Weibull, rainfall–runoff table, lake balance), then the FSF energy and momentum questions, then routing checks (SLR/Muskingum) and sketches.
  4. Every sketch: label axes and units, show yn and yc lines, mark controls, name the profile (M1, S3…) and show flow direction. Sketch marks are cheap if you're systematic.
  5. State assumptions out loud: hydrostatic pressure, no energy loss across short transitions, α = β = 1, steady flow, uniform rain, baseflow constant. The papers say to state any information you think is missing. Do it, then carry on.
  6. Never leave an MCQ blank. When options differ by a factor of 10 or 3.6, the question is testing units: check them.
